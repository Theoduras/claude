Velvet — Style Guide

The interface should
feel like the fabric.

Velvet pairs people the instant they're looking for each other. Its surfaces borrow the behaviour of crushed velvet: light pools on a crest, shadow gathers in the crease, and the nap changes as you brush it.

Foundation

Where the palette comes from

Two bolts of stretch velvet — one violet, one deep teal — sampled at three depths each: the specular crest, the mid pile, and the near-black inside a fold. Violet leads; teal answers. Nothing here is a flat brand colour, because velvet is never one colour.

Violet Crest #A855F7 Highlights, focus, glow
Violet #8A2BE2 Primary action
Violet Deep #3B0B66 Fold shadow, gradient base
Teal Crest #1DA6A2 Live state, incoming
Teal #12807F Secondary action
Teal Deep #06383B Received messages
Ink #0B0713 Page ground
Champagne #E8D3A9 Light itself — values, focus ring

The neutrals are violet-biased on purpose. Ink is not #0B0B0B — it carries a trace of the violet dye, the way the inside of a fold still reads as the fabric's colour. A pure grey next to these swatches looks like a mistake.

Signature

The velvet surface

The texture is drawn, not photographed — three stacked layers, no image requests, no fixed art direction that breaks at another viewport. Use it on the page ground and on panels. Never on small controls: at that size the grain turns to dirt.

Layer 1 — The pile

Overlapping soft radial gradients place the light. Violet pools top-left, teal answers top-right, and the deepest violet gathers along the bottom edge where cloth would bunch.

Layer 2 — The folds

Two conic gradients radiate from off-centre points, alternating white and black stops so pleats sweep out from a twist. A 26–28px blur keeps every crease edge soft — this is the step that decides whether it reads as cloth or as a hard geometric band.

Layer 3 — The nap

An inline SVG feTurbulence grain plus a 1px repeating gradient raked to 94°, composited in overlay. The grain is the fibre; the rake is the cut pile's direction.

Behaviour — The brush

Velvet changes colour when you stroke it, so primary buttons sweep a highlight across themselves on hover, and panels lift 1–3px. Motion is always this: light moving over a surface, never a surface bouncing.

Typography

A serif that undresses well

High-contrast serif for anything a person reads slowly — names, headlines, the one line that sets a mood. Grotesque sans for anything they operate. The tension between the two is the whole personality: intimate to read, clinical to use.

Display XL · serif 500 · −0.02em · 1.06 Feeling for a match
Display L · serif 500 · −0.016em Mia, 26 · Berlin
Display M · serif 500 — card titles, choice cards Long-term relationship
Body L · sans · 1.14rem — lede, subtitle Sign in and search — we pair you the moment someone is looking for you too.
Body M · sans · 1rem/1.62 — running copy, messages Distance must fit inside both radii. Nobody matches you unless you match them back.
Label XS · sans 700 · 0.16em · uppercase — every form label Search radius
Numeric · mono · tabular — ages, distances, counts 21 – 35 years · 149 km · 20 searching

Do

  • Keep running copy near 65 characters.
  • Let serif headlines break where the meaning breaks — use <br> deliberately.
  • Set every number that appears in a column as tabular.
  • Use champagne for values the eye hunts for: a distance, an age range, a count.

Don't

  • Set serif below 1rem — the contrast collapses and it reads as an accident.
  • Use the serif for buttons or labels. Controls are sans, always.
  • Stack more than two type sizes inside one card.
  • Centre body copy. Centre only the searching state, which has no reading to do.

Components

Parts, and what they promise

Violet acts. Teal responds. That split is load-bearing: a violet control changes something about you, a teal one belongs to the other person or to live state.

Buttons

Pill radius throughout — nothing in Velvet has a sharp corner. Primary carries the sheen sweep and a violet cast shadow; secondary is teal and flat; ghost is a hairline for anything that undoes. Hover a primary button to brush the pile.

Tags

Best fit Marriage Live

Violet tags classify a person; teal tags report system state. Uppercase, 0.1em tracked, never more than two per card.

Searching state

One breathing orb, 2.4s, and nothing else moving on the page. Waiting is the most emotional screen in the product — it earns the only ambient animation.

Inputs & sliders

Fields sit darker than the panel they're on — recessed, like a seam. Focus is a violet crest border plus a 3px soft ring; the slider thumb is a lit bead with a real glow. Every slider states its value in champagne, right-aligned to the label.

Messages

MiaYou picked the same three things I did. Suspicious.
YouOr fate. Drink on Thursday?
MiaFate it is.

Yours is violet on the right, theirs is teal on the left — the two bolts of cloth, one per person. The tail corner tightens to 0.3rem on the sender's side. Sender names are uppercase micro-labels at 70% opacity, never full strength.

Voice

Low, warm, and never coy

Velvet is a dating product, so the copy carries heat — but it states facts. The sexiness is in restraint and specificity, never in innuendo or exclamation marks.

Write it like this

  • “Feeling for a match” — the fabric verb, doing real work.
  • “Something softer than swiping.”
  • “Nobody fits every filter yet. You'd match 3 people if you widen your radius.”
  • “Only the two matched members can write here.”

Not like this

  • “Oops! No matches found 😢” — no apologies, no emoji as tone.
  • “Get ready to meet your soulmate!!” — no promises we can't keep.
  • “Invalid search parameters.” — say which one and how to fix it.
  • “Sultry singles near you” — innuendo reads cheap, which is the opposite of the brand.

Errors name the fix. Every failure message in Velvet answers two questions in one sentence: what stopped, and what to change. “Age range must run from 18 up to 120” beats “invalid input”, and the waiting screen quantifies the cost of each filter rather than shrugging.

Craft rules

Non-negotiables

Contrast

  • Body copy is #F7F1FB or #B7A6CB on ink — both clear AA.
  • Faint text is for labels and hints only, never for content someone must read.
  • Never put violet text on violet fabric; put white on it.

Focus & motion

  • Every control shows a visible focus state; the ring is champagne so it reads on both fabrics.
  • Transitions are 0.18s for state, 0.55s for the sheen sweep. Nothing else.
  • prefers-reduced-motion stops the orb, the sweep, and every lift.

Restraint

  • One glow per screen. If two things glow, neither is special.
  • Texture belongs to grounds and panels, not to buttons, tags, or inputs.
  • Champagne is light, not a third brand colour — it never fills a shape.
