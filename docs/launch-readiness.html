What Velvt still needs before real people can join

The infrastructure that makes a dating service survivable — recoverable accounts, a way to report someone, a lawful basis for the data — has now been built. What's left is mostly not code.

17 Resolved Built, tested and merged. Every Gate 1 and Gate 2 item.
3 Part done Plumbing built; each needs something that isn't code — legal copy, a provider, registered details.
10 Outstanding Gates 3 and 4. Of these, image scanning is the one that should not wait.
Status, August 2026

Everything in Gates 1 and 2 has been implemented across three commits on claude/app-launch-readiness-vc0yui, covered by 51 behaviour checks in check_auth.py. Findings below are marked Resolved where that is the case, with what was actually built. What remains is listed in the sequencing section at the end — and two of those cannot be closed by writing code at all.

Accounts, login, and staying logged in

Registration today is a username and a password. Nothing else is collected, which means nothing can be recovered, verified, or revoked.

There is no way to reset a forgotten password

Resolved
Was

No email, no phone, no recovery contact of any kind is stored. A user who forgets their password is locked out permanently, and you have no way to prove they own the account even if they write to you.

Add

Collect a verified email at registration. Reset via a single-use, time-limited, hashed token stored server-side. Invalidate every session on reset. This is the single biggest support burden you would otherwise ship.

Login does not survive closing the tab

Resolved
Was

session.permanent is never set, so Flask issues a browser-session cookie and PERMANENT_SESSION_LIFETIME is never configured. Close the browser and you are logged out. On mobile that happens constantly.

Add

A "keep me signed in" choice at login: session.permanent = True with a lifetime of ~30 days, refreshed on activity. Keep a short-lived session for people who decline.

Sessions cannot be revoked

Resolved
Was

Auth is a signed Flask cookie with no server-side record. There is no "sign out other devices", no session list, and no remedy for a stolen or borrowed phone. Rotating APP_SECRET_KEY is the only lever, and it logs out everyone at once.

Add

A sessions table keyed by an opaque token, with device label, IP, and last-seen. Let people see and end their sessions. Revoke all on password change or reset.

Nothing throttles login or registration

Resolved
Was

No rate limiting anywhere in the app. Credential stuffing against /login is free and unlimited, and /register will mint accounts as fast as a script can post. /api/places is also an unauthenticated proxy to a third-party geocoder, billable to you.

Add

Flask-Limiter backed by Redis (per-IP and per-account), progressive delays on failed logins, and a bot check on registration. Rate-limit /api/places and message sending too.

Password policy is below the 2026 baseline

Part done
Was

An 8-character minimum and nothing else. Werkzeug's hashing default is fine; the policy around it is not.

Add

NIST SP 800-63B-4 (final July 2025) keeps 8 as the floor but recommends 15, requires screening new passwords against a breach corpus, and drops composition rules and forced rotation. Screen against Have I Been Pwned's k-anonymity range API. Longer term, passkeys — Rev 4 recognises synced passkeys at AAL2 — are where consumer auth is going. Status: Floor kept at 8 with no composition rules or rotation, per SP 800-63B-4. Breach-list screening and passkeys are still outstanding.

No email verification, so no trustworthy identity

Resolved
Was

Accounts are anonymous by construction. A banned user re-registers in ten seconds under a new username.

Add

Verify the email before a profile can go live-searching. Keep a hash of banned identifiers so a ban actually holds.

Safety and moderation

This is the largest gap. There is currently no way for a user to report anyone, no way to block anyone, and no way for you to remove anyone. For a dating product that is the category-defining risk, and parts of it are a legal requirement rather than a feature choice.

No reporting mechanism

Resolved
Was

Nothing in the app lets a user flag a profile, a message, or a photo.

Add

DSA Article 16 requires every hosting provider offering services in the EU to run an easy, electronic notice-and-action mechanism, and to send the reporter a statement of reasons. The micro/small-enterprise exemption covers internal complaint handling, trusted flaggers and out-of-court settlement — it does not cover this. Report from a profile, a chat, and a photo, with a reason list and a free-text field.

No block, and unmatch is only available in the decision window

Resolved
Was

Unmatch exists only as a decision-phase button. Once a match goes active there is no way to get away from someone.

Add

Block from anywhere: ends the match, hides both profiles from each other, and excludes the pair from future try_pair() results permanently.

You cannot suspend or remove a user

Resolved
Was

users has is_admin and is_bot and no status column. The only admin tooling is /admin/profiles/new, which creates profiles. There is no queue, no case history, no action log.

Add

A users.status column (active / suspended / banned) enforced in login_required and in pairing, plus a reports table and a minimal admin queue: open reports, the reported content in context, and act/dismiss with a recorded reason.

No content moderation on messages or photos

Before scale
Was

send_message() stores any body verbatim with no length cap and no filtering. Photos are accepted as raw bytes with a MIME sniff and a size limit, and nothing else.

Add

A message length cap and a per-minute send limit. For images, scan on upload — CSAM detection is non-negotiable for a service where strangers exchange photos, and you should be talking to a provider before launch, not after your first incident.

No photo verification

Fast-follow
Was

Nothing links a profile photo to the person using the account.

Add

Selfie-to-profile-photo verification with a visible badge is now table stakes — Tinder, Bumble and Hinge all run some form of it, and it is the feature users cite most when choosing an app. It also gives you a lever short of a ban: restrict unverified accounts.

No safety guidance anywhere in the product

Resolved
Was

No safety centre, no advice about meeting in person, no scam warnings, no emergency resources.

Add

A safety page linked from the footer and the first match, covering meeting in public, telling a friend, and not moving to another platform or sending money. Romance-scam losses reported to the FTC ran to $1.16bn in the first nine months of 2025 — an in-context warning when a conversation turns to money is cheap and effective.

Age assurance

The city list points at Germany, Austria, Switzerland and the Netherlands. That is the jurisdiction where age assurance is tightening fastest, and a dating service is exactly the category regulators look at first.

Age is self-declared, optional, and collected after the account exists

Resolved
Was

Registration asks only for a username and password. Age lives on the profile, bounded to 18–39 by AGE_MIN_YEARS/AGE_MAX_YEARS only when the profile form is submitted. An account can exist, and reach the app, with no age at all.

Add

Ask for date of birth at registration, not age — store the DOB, derive the age. Hard-gate under-18s, and don't let a rejected attempt immediately retry with a different date.

No route to real age verification

Part done
Was

Self-declaration is the whole system.

Add

The Commission is pushing member states to roll out the EU age-verification app by the end of 2026, ahead of the EUDI wallet; it returns only an over-18 yes/no without identifying the user. Germany's child-and-youth-protection commission reports in summer 2026 and the Netherlands is legislating nationally. Build the seam now — an age_verified_at column and a pluggable provider — so you can drop the EU app in rather than re-architecting. Status: An age_verified_at column exists as the integration seam. No provider is wired in yet.

Privacy and legal

A dating profile that records gender and who you are seeking makes sexual orientation inferable. Under GDPR that is special category data, and the app currently has no consent, no policy, and no way to delete an account.

Special category data processed with no lawful basis

Resolved
Was

profiles.gender plus profiles.seeking reveals sexual orientation. searches stores precise lat/lng. No consent of any kind is captured.

Add

Article 9 prohibits processing this by default; you need an Article 9 exception and a separate Article 6 basis. In practice that means explicit, specific, freely given, withdrawable consent, captured at the point the data is entered and recorded with a timestamp. Note that the Norwegian DPA has held that using a dating app does not amount to manifestly making orientation public — so that exception is not available to you.

No account deletion and no data export

Resolved
Was

There is no delete route and no export route in app.py. Once someone joins, they cannot leave.

Add

Self-service deletion (Article 17) and export (Articles 15 and 20). ON DELETE CASCADE already covers profiles and photos; decide deliberately what happens to messages the other party still sees. A short grace period before hard deletion is normal and prevents rage-quit regret.

No terms, privacy policy, or imprint

Part done
Was

The landing page goes straight from "Start searching" to registration. No document is presented or accepted.

Add

Terms of service and a privacy policy, both linked from registration with an explicit acceptance step and a stored record of which version was accepted when. Germany (DDG §5) and Austria (ECG §5) require an Impressum; the DSA requires a published point of contact and clear terms. Add a cookie/consent notice if you ever add analytics. Status: Routes, versioned acceptance and the footer are built. The copy is placeholder and the imprint still needs the operator's registered details.

No retention limits

Before scale
Was

Messages, photos, ended matches and searches with precise coordinates persist forever.

Add

A written retention schedule and a job that enforces it — purge ended matches and their messages after a defined window, drop searches rows once resolved, and coarsen or discard stored coordinates.

Production security

Several of these are configuration defaults that are safe locally and dangerous the moment the app is public.

The admin account has a known default password

Resolved
Was

ADMIN_PASSWORD falls back to "admin12345" and init_db() creates the admin at import time. If APP_ADMIN_PASSWORD is not set in the deployment environment, a publicly reachable admin account exists with a password published in this repository.

Add

Refuse to start in production when APP_ADMIN_PASSWORD is unset, rather than falling back. Same treatment for AUTO_LOGIN — it currently logs every anonymous visitor in as admin, and nothing ties it to debug mode.

A missing secret key silently breaks sessions across workers

Resolved
Was

app.secret_key falls back to secrets.token_hex(32). The Dockerfile runs gunicorn with two workers, so each worker signs cookies with a different key. Users would be logged out at random depending on which worker answers.

Add

Hard-fail at startup if APP_SECRET_KEY is unset and the app is not in debug mode. The random fallback is the right call locally and a silent outage in production.

No CSRF protection on any POST

Resolved
Was

No tokens anywhere. Every state-changing endpoint is forgeable from another site: /profile/edit, /chat/<id>/send, /match/<id>/decide, /search/*, /logout.

Add

Flask-WTF's CSRFProtect with a token in every form and on the fetch-based endpoints. SameSite=Lax mitigates the classic case but is not a substitute, and is only set when CANONICAL_HOST happens to be configured.

Cookie hardening depends on an unrelated env var

Resolved
Was

SESSION_COOKIE_SECURE, HTTPONLY and SAMESITE are set only inside if CANONICAL_HOST:. A deployment that hasn't mapped a domain yet runs over HTTPS with none of them.

Add

Key the hardening off "is this production" rather than off the canonical-host redirect. The instinct — not setting Secure on plain-http localhost — is right; the trigger is wrong.

No security headers

Before scale
Was

X-Content-Type-Options is set on /photo/<id> and nowhere else. No CSP, no HSTS, no frame-ancestors, no Referrer-Policy.

Add

An after_request hook applying a Content-Security-Policy, Strict-Transport-Security, frame-ancestors 'none', and Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin to every response.

Debug defaults on, and the design lab is public

Resolved
Was

FLASK_DEBUG defaults to 1 in the __main__ block. Gunicorn bypasses it, so this is latent rather than live — but the default is the wrong way round. /lab serves the mockup file unauthenticated. There is also no MAX_CONTENT_LENGTH, so oversized uploads are parsed before PHOTO_MAX_BYTES rejects them.

Add

Default debug to off and opt in locally. Gate /lab behind admin or drop it from the production image. Set MAX_CONTENT_LENGTH so the request is refused before it is read into memory.

No logging or alerting for security events

Fast-follow
Was

Gunicorn access logs only. Failed logins, admin actions and report submissions leave no trace.

Add

Structured auth events (login success/failure, password change, admin action, report filed) and an alert on failure spikes. You need this to answer "what happened" after your first incident.

Onboarding and information

Velvt's core mechanic — a 20-second reveal, five minutes of chat, then both people decide — is genuinely unusual and genuinely good. Right now nobody is told about it before it happens to them.

Registration drops you into a long profile form

Before scale
Was

/register redirects straight to /profile/edit — one dense form covering name, age, gender, seeking, location, six physical traits, six preference fields, bio, interests, hobbies, wants, needs and relationship type.

Add

Break it into steps with visible progress: the essentials first (name, DOB, gender, seeking, location, one photo), the rest optional and improvable later. Let people reach the app before asking for the long tail.

The timed-match mechanic is never explained

Before scale
Was

A first-time user starts a search with no idea that a match opens with a 20-second reveal, runs for five minutes, and then ends unless both people continue. The countdown appears without context.

Add

A short "how a match works" screen before the first search, and a one-time explainer on the first reveal. This is your differentiator — the product should say so out loud.

An empty profile can go live-searching

Fast-follow
Was

Nothing requires a name, a photo, or a bio before searching. The person on the other side of a five-minute conversation may have nothing to look at.

Add

Require a minimum viable profile — name, age, gender, seeking, one photo — before the first search, with a completeness meter to encourage the rest.

No help, FAQ, or way to contact you

Fast-follow
Was

The nav has Live search, Chats and Profile. There is no footer, no help, no support address, no FAQ.

Add

A footer with help, safety, terms, privacy, imprint and a monitored support address. The DSA also requires a published point of contact, so this is partly the legal work above.

No settings screen

Fast-follow
Was

There is nowhere to change a password, manage sessions, adjust notification or privacy preferences, export data, or delete the account.

Add

One settings page that gathers account, privacy, safety and data controls. Most of the blocking items above need somewhere to live, and this is it.

What's left

Gates 1 and 2 are done. These are the remaining items, in the order they matter.

Cannot be closed by writing code

Two blockers need a person, not a commit.

  • The legal copy. Terms, privacy and imprint exist as routes with versioned acceptance recorded against them, and the text describes what the app genuinely does — but it is placeholder and marked as such in the pages themselves. It needs a lawyer.
  • The imprint's registered details. DDG §5 and ECG §5 want the operator's real legal name, address, register number and responsible person. None of it can be invented.
  • A monitored support address. SUPPORT_EMAIL is published in the footer and named as the DSA point of contact, so somebody has to be reading it.

Before it grows

These break at a few thousand users rather than a few dozen.

  • Image scanning on upload. The one item here that should not wait for scale — CSAM detection on a service where strangers exchange photos is a talk-to-a-provider-before-launch problem, not a fast-follow.
  • Message length caps to go with the send rate limit already in place
  • Security headers: CSP, HSTS, frame-ancestors, Referrer-Policy
  • Breach-list password screening, then passkeys
  • A retention schedule and a purge job — the deletion purge endpoint exists and is the obvious place to extend
  • Structured auth logging and alerting on failure spikes

Trust and onboarding

Quality work, none of it blocking.

  • Selfie photo verification with a visible badge
  • Stepped onboarding, and a "how a match works" explainer before the first search
  • A minimum viable profile before searching, with a completeness meter
  • An FAQ to sit alongside the safety page
  • Wire in the EU age-verification app once it ships — the age_verified_at seam is already there
